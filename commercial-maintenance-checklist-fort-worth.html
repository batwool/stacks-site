Commercial property maintenance in Fort Worth: an annual checklist

The city-required tasks, seasonal work and routine upkeep that keep Fort Worth offices, retail centers and multi-unit buildings compliant and running.

By Jeremy Spann, co-owner of Stacks Property Services. Published November 16, 2026. Last updated November 16, 2026. 8 minute read.

Quick answer

Every Fort Worth commercial property needs an annual test of each backflow prevention assembly by a city-registered tester, working rain and freeze sensors on its irrigation system, and irrigation limited to Tuesdays and Fridays outside 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Add freeze protection for fire sprinklers in winter and a seasonal schedule for roofs, HVAC, lighting, doors and common areas.

What maintenance does Fort Worth require for commercial properties?

Fort Worth requires annual testing of every backflow prevention assembly on commercial property. Backflow assemblies keep water from your building’s plumbing, irrigation or fire lines from flowing back into the city’s drinking water. They are mechanical devices that wear out, so the city requires a test every year and after any installation, repair or relocation.

  • Who can test: a licensed backflow prevention assembly tester (BPAT) who is registered with the City of Fort Worth. Testers are independent contractors, not city staff.
  • How results are filed: testers submit reports online through Vepo, LLC, the company the city uses for annual testing notices and report tracking.
  • Fire sprinkler lines: backflow assemblies on fire sprinkler systems must be tested by a BPAT who works full time for a fire sprinkler company licensed with the State Fire Marshal’s Office.
  • Irrigation: rain and freeze sensors are required on all new and existing commercial irrigation systems and must be kept in working order.
  • Watering days: commercial properties may only run irrigation on Tuesdays and Fridays, and never between 10 a.m. and 6 p.m.
  • Permits: installing, relocating, replacing or removing a backflow assembly requires a plumbing backflow permit from Fort Worth Development Services.

What should a seasonal maintenance schedule look like in Fort Worth?

Plan maintenance around North Texas weather: freezes in winter, storms in spring, extreme heat in summer, and preparation in fall.

SeasonPriority tasks
Winter (December to February)Keep heat on overnight and on weekends in buildings with fire sprinklers when a freeze is forecast. Drain irrigation, insulate exposed pipes and backflow assemblies, and confirm after-hours access to shut-off valves.
Spring (March to May)Restart irrigation and test rain and freeze sensors. Inspect roofs, gutters and downspouts after storms. Check exterior lighting and parking lot drainage.
Summer (June to September)Service HVAC and replace filters. Watch for foundation movement around the building, including new cracks and sticking doors. Touch up exterior paint and sealant.
Fall (October to November)Schedule backflow testing if it isn’t done, clean gutters, check weatherstripping and door closers, and prepare winterizing supplies.

What routine upkeep should happen monthly or quarterly?

Routine walk-throughs catch small problems while they are still cheap to fix. A monthly or quarterly visit should cover the items tenants and customers notice first.

  • Interior and exterior lighting, including parking lot and stairwell lights
  • Doors, locks, closers and panic hardware
  • Restroom fixtures, leaks and running toilets
  • Ceiling tiles and walls for water stains that point to roof or plumbing leaks
  • Common-area paint, flooring and trim
  • Landscaping, irrigation heads and drainage near the building
  • HVAC filters and visible condensate leaks

Why is preventive maintenance worth it for commercial buildings?

Preventive maintenance turns emergency repairs into scheduled ones. A cracked irrigation line found in spring costs a part and an hour; the same line bursting during a freeze can flood a storefront and close a business for days.

It also protects compliance. The Fort Worth Water Department sends annual backflow testing notices, and one Fort Worth plumbing company notes that ignoring them can lead to water service shutoff. Keeping a written schedule, with test reports and inspection records in one place, makes renewals and insurance questions simple.

Frequently asked questions

How often do backflow preventers need to be tested in Fort Worth?

Every year for all backflow assemblies on commercial property, and also after installation, repair or relocation. Fort Worth also requires annual testing of any assembly protecting against a health hazard, regardless of property type.

Who can test a backflow preventer in Fort Worth?

Only a licensed backflow prevention assembly tester registered with the City of Fort Worth. Fire sprinkler line assemblies must be tested by a BPAT employed full time by a licensed fire sprinkler company. The city links to a list of registered testers through Vepo, LLC.

What days can commercial properties water in Fort Worth?

Commercial properties may run irrigation systems on Tuesdays and Fridays only, and never between 10 a.m. and 6 p.m. Drought stages can add stricter limits.

Are rain and freeze sensors required on commercial sprinkler systems?

Yes. Fort Worth requires rain and freeze sensors on all new and existing commercial irrigation systems, and they must be kept in working order.

How do I protect a fire sprinkler system during a freeze?

Keep the building heated overnight and on weekends whenever subfreezing temperatures are forecast. The City of Fort Worth gives this advice for all commercial buildings with fire sprinklers.
