How often should you water your foundation in Fort Worth?

What Fort Worth’s clay soil does to a slab foundation, how much to water by season, and what the city’s watering rules allow.

By Jeremy Spann, co-owner of Stacks Property Services. Published November 16, 2026. Last updated November 16, 2026. 7 minute read.

Quick answer

Water your foundation in Fort Worth whenever the soil next to the slab is dry or pulling away. In a hot, dry summer that can mean every day; in winter it means far less often. Use a soaker hose or drip line, which Fort Worth allows on any day at any time. Sprinkler systems are limited to two assigned days a week and never between 10 a.m. and 6 p.m.

Why do Fort Worth foundations need watering?

Fort Worth foundations need watering because most homes sit on expansive clay soil that swells when wet and shrinks when dry. When the soil shrinks in summer heat, it can leave voids under the slab and let the house settle. When heavy rain returns, the soil swells and can push the foundation back up.

Dallas and Fort Worth sit in the Blackland Prairie, a region known for this expansive black clay. Texas A&M AgriLife Extension publishes guidance on maintaining foundations on expansive clay soils for exactly this reason. The goal of foundation watering is not to make the soil wet. It is to keep the moisture level steady all year so the soil stops moving.

How often should I water my foundation in each season?

Water based on how the soil looks and feels, not a fixed calendar. The soil next to your foundation should be moist, never muddy and never cracked.

SeasonHow oftenWhat to watch for
Summer (June to September)Several times a week, and daily during long stretches near 100°F with no rainSoil cracks, gaps between soil and slab
Spring and fallAdjust to rainfall; skip watering after a good soaking rainStanding water near the slab after storms
Winter (December to February)Much less often. One DFW foundation company recommends watering 75 to 80% less often than in summerA dry winter still shrinks clay, so keep watering if the soil is dry

A quick test: push a long screwdriver into the soil about a foot from the slab. If it goes in easily, the moisture is fine. If it won’t go in, or the soil has pulled away from the foundation, it needs water.

What does the City of Fort Worth allow for foundation watering?

Fort Worth allows hand watering, soaker hoses and drip irrigation on any day and at any time, which makes them the best tools for foundation watering. The city’s watering ordinance specifically exempts these three methods from the twice-per-week schedule that applies to lawns, landscape and structural foundations.

  • Sprinkler and irrigation systems may run only on your two assigned days each week.
  • No sprinkler or irrigation system watering between 10 a.m. and 6 p.m., year round.
  • A hose with a hose-end sprinkler is not considered hand watering. Hand watering means a hand-held hose with a nozzle.
  • Drought stages can tighten these rules. Check Fort Worth Water’s current restrictions during a drought.

How do I set up a soaker hose around my foundation?

Lay a soaker hose around the full perimeter of the house, a short distance from the slab, and run it on a timer. This delivers slow, even moisture that clay can absorb without runoff.

  1. Lay the soaker hose roughly 12 to 18 inches out from the foundation on all sides, not touching the slab.
  2. Cover the hose with mulch to slow evaporation and keep it out of sight.
  3. Connect a battery hose timer at the spigot so watering happens on schedule, including when you travel.
  4. Run it for a short session, then check the soil. Stop if water pools or the soil turns muddy.
  5. Adjust the schedule each season using the table above.

Watering only one side of the house can cause uneven movement, so cover every side, including the shaded side that dries more slowly.

What else protects a foundation on clay soil?

Drainage matters as much as watering. Water that pools against the slab after a storm swells the clay just as damaging as drought shrinks it.

  • Grade the soil away from the house so rainwater can’t puddle against the foundation. Flower beds should slope away too.
  • Extend downspouts several feet from the slab and keep gutters clear.
  • Plant shrubs and trees at a distance. Roots pull moisture from the soil under the slab. One DFW foundation repair company recommends planting large shrubs 4.5 to 5 feet from the foundation and large trees much farther away.
  • Fix plumbing leaks quickly. A slow leak under or beside the slab can swell the clay in one spot.

What are the signs my foundation is moving?

Doors and windows that start sticking, and new cracks above door frames, are the most common early signs of foundation movement in Fort Worth homes.

  • Diagonal cracks in drywall at the corners of doors and windows
  • Stair-step cracks in brick on the outside of the house
  • Gaps between baseboards and the floor, or between the soil and the slab
  • Floors that feel uneven or doors that swing open on their own

If cracks keep growing, have a licensed foundation engineer or foundation repair company evaluate the house. Watering prevents movement; it doesn’t repair a foundation that has already failed.

Frequently asked questions

Should I water my foundation in winter in Fort Worth?

Yes, if the soil is dry. Clay soil shrinks in a dry winter just as it does in summer. Water much less often than in summer and stop if the ground is wet or frozen.

Can I water my foundation during Fort Worth watering restrictions?

Yes. Under Fort Worth’s year-round rules, hand watering, soaker hoses and drip irrigation are allowed any day and any time. Sprinkler systems follow the twice-per-week schedule and can’t run between 10 a.m. and 6 p.m. Check for added limits during drought stages.

Can I overwater my foundation?

Yes. Saturated clay swells and can lift the slab, causing damage similar to drought. If water pools or the soil turns muddy, turn the water off and wait for the soil to dry to a moist level.

The soil around my house already has wide cracks. What should I do?

Bring the moisture back slowly with short, frequent soaker hose sessions instead of flooding the cracks. Sudden heavy watering can cause rapid swelling. If you see new interior cracks, have the foundation evaluated.

Do landlords need to water foundations at rental homes?

Someone should, and the lease should say who. Many Fort Worth landlords put soaker hoses on a timer at their rentals so foundation watering doesn’t depend on the tenant.
